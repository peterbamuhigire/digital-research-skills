## Observe and baseline

The portfolio already had anti-slop mechanisms, but coverage was uneven. SRS, business plans,
social media, proposals, engineering, and Digital Research had production/audit skills; website,
design, and accounting had adjacent gates; Linux, Windows administration, and political writing had
no dedicated anti-slop skill. Existing rules emphasised banned vocabulary, unsupported claims,
genericness, visual convergence, and template uniformity. The seven machine errors were not named as
a shared review contract, and no portfolio validator checked their coverage.

## Select and experiment

- **Aim:** catch polished output that imposes reading or interaction cost without adding meaning.
- **Hypothesis:** a shared seven-check gate with domain adapters will reduce semantic duplication,
  mannered structure, and hollow sections without blocking functional repetition.
- **Change:** add the dated machine-error reference, central production/audit instructions,
  domain-specific adapters, a deterministic 12-engine coverage validator, and pressure fixtures.
- **Owner:** engine maintainers, coordinated through Digital Research.
- **Stop condition:** any adapter blocks required traceability, safety, accessibility, legal wording,
  accounting auditability, or deterministic implementation; remove or narrow that adapter.
- **Rollback:** revert the dated reference, adapters, validator, and fixtures; retain prior gates.

## Standardise and teach

The dated reference is now the cross-engine source of truth. Existing gates link to it and carry
domain exceptions. Linux, Windows, and political routers now expose the gate explicitly. Digital
Research's release instructions require the coverage validator after anti-slop or cross-engine gate
changes.
