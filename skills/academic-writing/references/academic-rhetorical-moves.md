# Academic Rhetorical Moves

Use this guide when drafting any section of a paper, thesis or dissertation and the question is
"what does this paragraph need to do, and how do skilled writers signal it?". It maps each
section to the moves readers and examiners expect, gives decision rules, and supplies
engine-written frames to adapt. For reporting verbs and hedges, load
`reporting-verbs-and-hedging.md`.

## Inputs

- The section being drafted and its job in the argument.
- The note cards and evidence for that section (`source-away-note-cards.md`).
- The discipline's conventions on voice (some license "we" and "I"; many do not).

## Decision rules

1. **Choose the move before the wording.** Name the move (for example "identify the gap") in a
   margin note, then write. A paragraph without an identifiable move is usually padding.
2. **Frames are scaffolding, not content.** A generic frame (at most about nine words, no more
   than four content words, expressing no one else's viewpoint) may be reused as a skeleton. Any
   phrase with a distinctive construction, or that carries another author's position, is a
   quotation and must be cited or rewritten.
3. **Vary the frame.** Do not open three paragraphs with the same construction; examiners and
   plagiarism tools both notice template prose.
4. **Content first.** Replace every placeholder with a specific claim, population, date and
   source. A frame with vague content is still vague.

## Moves by section

### Introduction

| Move | What it does | Engine frames to adapt |
|---|---|---|
| Establish territory | Shows the topic matters to a field or practice | "X shapes how [population] [does Y]." / "Decisions about X now affect [scale]." |
| Time-frame the interest | Shows why now | "Since [year/event], attention to X has grown because [reason]." |
| Name the problem | States what is not working or not known | "Yet [specific phenomenon] remains unexplained." |
| Identify the gap | Shows the literature has not answered it | "Studies of X have focused on [A]; [B] has not been examined." |
| State the aim or question | Commits the study | "This study asks whether [X] affects [Y] among [population]." |
| State significance | Says who benefits | "An answer would let [actor] decide [decision] on evidence." |
| Outline | Signposts the structure | "Section 2 reviews ...; Section 3 sets out the method ..." |

### Literature review

| Move | Engine frames |
|---|---|
| Characterise a body of work | "Work on X falls into [two/three] strands: ..." |
| Trace development | "Early studies treated X as [A]; later work reframed it as [B]." |
| Group multiple sources | "Several studies report [finding] (Author, Year; Author, Year)." |
| State settled knowledge | "There is broad agreement that [claim] (sources)." |
| Expose inconsistency | "These studies disagree on [point]; [A] finds ..., whereas [B] finds ..." |
| Position the study | "Building on [Author (Year)], this study extends the analysis to [context]." |

### Methods

Describe what was done, to whom, with what, and how it was analysed, in the past tense, in
enough detail to replicate. Frames: "Data were collected through [instrument] between [dates]."
/ "Participants were recruited from [frame] using [method]." / "Responses were analysed with
[technique] in [software]." / "Ethical approval was granted by [body], reference [number]."
The justification for each choice belongs to
`../../academic-reporting-standards/references/methodology-justification-checklist.md`.

### Results

Point to the evidence, then state the pattern without interpreting it. Frames: "Table 2 shows
[pattern]." / "[N] of [N] respondents reported [X]." / "The difference between [A] and [B] was
[statistically significant / not significant] ([test, value])."

### Discussion

| Move | Engine frames |
|---|---|
| Restate the main finding | "The central result is that [finding]." |
| Compare with prior work | "This agrees with [Author (Year)] but diverges from [Author (Year)] on [point]." |
| Explain, with calibrated caution | "One explanation is [mechanism]; [alternative] cannot be ruled out." |
| Unexpected result | "Contrary to the hypothesis, [result]." |
| Limitations | "Because [limitation], the findings apply to [scope] only." |

### Conclusion

Return to the aim ("This study set out to establish ..."), state what is now known, give
implications for practice or policy, and name specific further research. No new evidence.

## General functions (any section)

- **Define:** "In this study, X means [operational definition]." Define before use; if a central
  term cannot be defined, the study is not ready.
- **Classify:** "X takes [n] forms: [a], [b], [c], distinguished by [criterion]."
- **Compare:** "Whereas A [does], B [does]." / "A and B differ in [dimension]."
- **Signal cause:** therefore, consequently, as a result (only when causation is supported).
- **Signal contrast:** however, by contrast, conversely.
- **Add:** moreover, in addition, a further point.
- **Exemplify:** for example, a case in point is.
- **Describe trends and quantities:** use exact figures first, then the verbal description
  ("rose from 12% to 31% between 2019 and 2024, more than doubling").

## Style checks

Evidence-based, cautious to the right degree, formal (no contractions or slang), impersonal
unless the discipline licenses the first person, precise (the technical word over the everyday
one: "considerable" over "a lot of", "approach" over "way of doing"), concise, objective (no
rhetorical questions or emotive language), and structured (topic sentence, evidence, link).
Avoid excessive nominalisation.

## Original worked example

Gap paragraph for a Ugandan health-financing thesis:
"Studies of community health insurance in East Africa have concentrated on enrolment (sources).
Far fewer examine whether enrolled households in rural Uganda actually use the scheme when ill,
and none has compared use across schemes run by faith-based and district facilities. This
thesis asks whether facility ownership affects utilisation among enrolled households in Masaka
and Lira districts."
Moves: characterise literature, identify gap, state question. No sentence is borrowed.

## Checklist

- [ ] Every paragraph has a named move.
- [ ] Frames varied; none longer than about nine words reused verbatim.
- [ ] Placeholders replaced with specific content and sources.
- [ ] Voice matches the discipline.
- [ ] Discussion separates finding, comparison, explanation and limitation.

Sources: Swales (1990) *Genre Analysis*; Morley (2017) *Academic Phrasebank*; Davis and Morley
(2015) "Phrasal intertextuality", *Journal of Second Language Writing* 28. Frames are the
engine's own.
