# Growth, Profit, and Disruption Analysis

[Owning skill](../SKILL.md) | Also used by `report-and-proposal-craft` (growth strategy report)
and `knowledge-productization` (commercial decision aids)

Load this when a research output must judge how a business grows, where profit leaks, or
whether a change is transformative or incremental: growth strategy reports, commercial white
papers, business-plan evidence, SaaS/platform diagnostics, and proposal recommendations.

## Inputs

- The business model in one paragraph: customer, problem, offer, price, channel, delivery.
- Whatever evidence exists: revenue by segment, funnel or event data, cohort retention,
  pricing history, cost-to-serve, customer interviews. Missing data is recorded as a gap,
  never estimated silently.
- The decision the reader must make and by when.

## Procedure

1. **Map the growth system, not a channel list.** Trace customer -> problem -> positioning ->
   offer -> acquisition -> activation -> retention -> referral -> revenue -> delivery ->
   data -> management cadence. Mark each link as evidenced, assumed, or unknown.
2. **Name the growth engine.** The one loop that actually brings and keeps customers (for
   example referral, sales-led, content, product-led, partner). If none is evident, that is
   the headline finding.
3. **Find the binding constraint.** Demand, conversion, retention, price, delivery capacity,
   trust, data, or execution. Recommend work on the constraint before optimising anything else.
4. **Define the value proof.** Which user behaviour or metric shows the customer got value
   (the activation event), and does the data capture it?
5. **Decompose profit.** Treat price, packaging, offer mix, conversion, retention, purchase
   frequency, gross margin, and cost-to-serve as separate levers. Several modest,
   evidenced improvements are more credible than one dramatic lever; show the arithmetic.
6. **Test for transformation.** Ask whether the change alters the basis of competition
   (access, delivery, pricing model, journey, ownership, speed, operating model) or merely
   improves the current model. Assess readiness: leadership, incentives, talent, systems and
   data, customer experience, technology, milestones, feedback metrics.
7. **Challenge the recommendation** (run through `critical-reasoning-and-argument`):
   - What assumption, if false, makes this fail?
   - What would a competitor do after reading it?
   - What would a customer reject as inconvenient or risky?
   - What would a regulator, supplier, investor, or employee notice?
   - What would we recommend with half the budget?
   - What would make the current model obsolete?
8. **Close with an experiment plan and a dashboard owner**, not a slogan.

## Separations the report must keep

| Keep apart | Why |
|---|---|
| Revenue growth vs profit growth | Revenue can rise while contribution falls |
| Gross margin vs contribution margin | Contribution includes variable acquisition and service cost |
| Acquisition economics vs retention economics | Different levers, owners, and time horizons |
| Price tests vs price opinions | Survey willingness-to-pay is weak evidence next to observed behaviour |
| Cost reduction vs value improvement | Cutting cost-to-serve can erode retention |

## Digital-product evidence set

For SaaS, platforms, marketplaces, AI products, and apps request or build: an event
taxonomy, an activation definition, a retention cohort view, a KPI tree from north-star metric
to team levers, an experiment backlog with hypothesis and guardrail metric, and a list of
product-analytics gaps. Absent instrumentation is reported as a finding.

## Output checklist

- [ ] Growth engine named, with evidence status.
- [ ] Binding constraint identified and justified.
- [ ] Profit levers decomposed with arithmetic and sources.
- [ ] Transformation vs incremental judgement stated with readiness gaps.
- [ ] Strongest objection shown and answered.
- [ ] Experiments listed with metric, guardrail, owner, and stop rule.
- [ ] Dashboard owner and review cadence named.

## Worked example (original; figures are illustrative, not data)

A Kampala school-fees payment platform asks why growth stalled at 140 schools.

- Generic: "Invest in digital marketing and partnerships to accelerate growth."
- Senior: "Acquisition is not the constraint. 71% of onboarded schools process fees in term
  one, but only 38% do so in term two (platform records, 2025-2026). Bursars revert to bank
  deposit slips when parents face mobile-money charges above UGX 2,500. Retention, driven by
  parent-side fees, is the binding constraint. Test a school-absorbed flat fee in 20 schools for
  one term; guardrail: take rate per transaction may not fall below break-even. Owner: head of
  partnerships; reviewed weekly."

## Failure modes

- A channel list presented as a strategy.
- "Disruptive" used for incremental improvement.
- A profit claim without margin or cost-to-serve data.
- Recommendations with no owner, metric, or stop rule.
