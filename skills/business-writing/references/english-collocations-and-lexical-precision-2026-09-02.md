# English Output Overlay - Digital Research

[Owning skill](../SKILL.md) | Canonical study: `C:\wamp64\www\digital-research-skills\docs\continuous-improvement\english-collocations-and-lexical-precision-2026-09-02.md`

Apply this overlay to reports, briefs, source notes, synthesis, and research
correspondence. The standard is polished, literate, exact, and restrained: clear
judgement without pomp, generic filler, or artificial informality.

- Use British English by default, retaining source quotations, proper names, official terms, and the target publication's approved style.
- Separate fact, paraphrase, synthesis, inference, recommendation, assumption, and gap. Language polish never upgrades weak evidence.
- Prefer the ordinary accurate word. Check synonym sense, collocation, grammatical frame, connotation, register, and audience before choosing an advanced word.
- Use intensifiers only when the evidence earns them; otherwise state the number, comparison, mechanism, boundary, or uncertainty.
- Avoid idioms, slogans, stock connectors, vague adjectives, repeated paragraph shapes, false balance, and fashionable AI phrasing.
- Proof names, dates, figures, quotations, citations, links, terminology, spelling, and claim-source alignment. Read aloud where practical.

Record reader, purpose, locale/register, source status, terms checked, inference
labels, language/proof status, unresolved gaps, reviewer, and date. The supplied
books are durable language concepts, not current research, legal, policy, technology,
or standards authority.
