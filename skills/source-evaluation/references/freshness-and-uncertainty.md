# Source freshness and uncertainty

Apply this reference in addition to the credibility ladder. Freshness is claim-specific: a source can be authoritative for a historical fact and unusable for a current command, price, law, product capability, or safety instruction.

## Freshness classes

| Class | Typical use | Required treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Stable | Definitions, historical events, durable conceptual models | Record publication date and confirm that the claim is genuinely stable |
| Context-bound | Case studies, organisational practices, regional evidence, benchmarks | Preserve setting, population, method, and transfer limits |
| Time-sensitive | Laws, regulations, vendor capabilities, prices, security guidance, platform behaviour, current events | Verify against a current authoritative source before publication |
| Partial or early release | Incomplete manuscript, preview, missing chapters, draft | Record completeness; use only the available material and label it partial |
| Unusable | Corrupt extraction, image-only notice, missing provenance, unreadable content | Quarantine; do not infer or reconstruct content |

## Uncertainty register

For each material source or claim, record:

- provenance and access path;
- publication, revision, and access dates when known;
- completeness and extraction/media integrity;
- claim scope and freshness class;
- source tier, independence, and conflicts;
- what was checked, what was not checked, and why;
- decision impact if the claim is wrong;
- next verification trigger, owner, and due date.

Use the status vocabulary `verified`, `supported`, `contested`, `unverified`, `unassessed`, `partial`, `historical-only`, `corrupted`, or `rejected`. Never convert `unassessed` to `verified` because a source looks plausible.

## Admission rules

1. Admit a stable claim only when its context and intended wording fit.
2. Admit a context-bound claim with its setting and transfer limitation visible.
3. For a time-sensitive claim, require current-source verification or publish it as an explicit gap.
4. For partial sources, cite only available sections and label the source as partial in the registry.
5. Reject corrupted or unreadable material; do not fill gaps from memory or another copy without recording the new provenance.
