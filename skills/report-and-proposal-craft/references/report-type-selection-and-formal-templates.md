# Report Type Selection and Formal Templates

Use this guide to pick the right report type before drafting, and to build the two most
demanding formal artifacts: the formal research report and the business plan. General report and
proposal craft (reader map, executive summary, recommendations) lives in
`report-and-proposal-structure.md`.

## Inputs

- The question the report answers and who commissioned it.
- Whether the reader must act, and whether money, contracts or regulators are involved.
- The evidence base: primary data, secondary sources, instruments, datasets.
- Any mandated template (lender, funder, regulator, university, donor).

## Decision rule 1: pick one type

| Type | Use when the reader needs | Signature element |
|---|---|---|
| Informational | Facts without interpretation | No conclusions section |
| Analytical | Interpretation, no required action | Conclusions, no recommendations |
| Recommendation | A course of action with rationale | Numbered, owned, dated, costed recommendations |
| Progress / status | Work against plan | RAG status, variances, next-period plan |
| Feasibility | A go / no-go on a proposed initiative | Pre-agreed criteria scored per option |
| Justification | Defence of a past or proposed decision | Alternatives weighed against criteria |
| Audit / compliance | Conformance to a standard | Findings ranked by severity, remediation plan |
| Formal research | An answer to a defined business question | Replicable method section |
| Business plan | Funding or approval for a venture | Market analysis, financial projections, sensitivity |

Mixing types (an audit body with a sales-style recommendation, a feasibility study without
criteria) weakens both. If the brief genuinely needs two, produce two documents or two clearly
separated parts.

## Decision rule 2: formal or informal

Treat the report as formal if any of these holds: external recipient; a contractual or financial
commitment follows; longer than 10 pages; several readers with conflicting interests; audit or
regulatory context. Otherwise use memo or letter format.

## Decision rule 3: scale front and back matter to the document

| Element | Include when |
|---|---|
| Cover and title page | Every formal report |
| Transmittal letter or memo | External or commissioned work |
| Copy of the commissioning document | An external party commissioned the work |
| Contents | Longer than 5 pages |
| List of figures and tables | Three or more figures or tables |
| Executive summary | Longer than 10 pages, or any decision-grade report |
| Glossary | Specialised vocabulary |
| References | Any cited external work |
| Appendices | Support the body refers to but does not need inline |
| Index | Longer than 50 pages |

A 12-page report with a three-page transmittal letter and a two-page list of tables is inflated.

## Procedure: formal research report

1. **Front matter** as scaled above. The transmittal states purpose, scope, the principal
   finding, and an offer to discuss.
2. **Executive summary**: situation, method, findings, recommendations; one page up to 30 pages,
   two pages above that.
3. **Introduction**: background, problem statement, scope, limitations, definitions, brief
   method, plan of the report.
4. **Method**, to a replicability standard (next section).
5. **Findings**, one theme per subsection, each with its evidence and an anchored visual.
6. **Analysis**: what the findings mean for the question, alternative readings, limitations.
7. **Conclusions**: answers to the research questions; no new evidence.
8. **Recommendations**: action, owner, date, cost, rank.
9. **Back matter**: references in one citation style, appendices (instruments, raw tables,
   calculations, glossary), index if long.

### Replicability standard for the method section

A competent reader could repeat the study from the description. It states: design (descriptive,
exploratory, causal, longitudinal); population, sampling frame, sample size and selection;
primary versus secondary sources; instruments (copies in an appendix); collection procedure and
dates; analysis methods, software and thresholds; ethics (consent, confidentiality, conflicts);
and what the study cannot conclude.

### Findings, conclusions, recommendations

| Artifact | Answers | Voice |
|---|---|---|
| Finding | What the data shows | Descriptive, neutral |
| Conclusion | What it means for the question | Interpretive, no new evidence |
| Recommendation | What to do | Imperative, owned, dated, costed |

A "finding" that recommends, or a "conclusion" that introduces new data, is the most common
formal-report defect.

## Procedure: business plan

1. Cover and title page with version and confidentiality marking.
2. Executive summary on one page: opportunity, solution, market, traction, team, ask, use of
   funds.
3. Company description: mission, legal form, ownership, history.
4. Market analysis: industry, segments, size and growth with sources, competitors.
5. Organisation and management: structure, key people, board or advisers.
6. Products or services: what is sold, lifecycle stage, intellectual property.
7. Marketing and sales: positioning, pricing, channels, acquisition cost, customer lifetime value.
8. Funding request: amount, instrument, use of funds, later rounds.
9. Financial projections: history if any, three to five years of profit and loss, cash flow and
   balance sheet, an assumptions table, and a downside sensitivity case.
10. Appendices: CVs, detailed financials, letters of intent, market data, registrations.

For finance-standard questions (IFRS presentation, tax), route to the finance engine; for
investor-grade plans, route to the business-plan engine.

## Body or appendix?

Body: anything the reader needs once to follow the argument (headline finding, summary chart,
one telling quotation, the recommendation). Appendix: anything referenced repeatedly or too
detailed for the flow (complete datasets, full transcripts, the costing model, the full
glossary). An appendix that holds material the argument depends on is a graveyard.

## Visuals

One message per visual, stated in the title. Number figures and tables consecutively; figure
captions below, table titles above; a source line on every visual, including "Source: survey by
the authors". Line for trend, bar for category comparison, table for exact values; pies only
for composition with six or fewer slices and never in 3-D. Refer to each visual in the text
before it appears.

## Length guide

| Type | Typical pages |
|---|---|
| Progress | 1 to 5 |
| Informational | 2 to 10 |
| Recommendation | 5 to 20 |
| Analytical | 5 to 25 |
| Feasibility | 10 to 30 |
| Audit / compliance | 15 to 50 |
| Business plan | 25 to 50 |
| Formal research | 30 to 80 |

These are working norms, not rules; a mandated template overrides them.

## Tone

Third person for findings ("the data show"); "we recommend" acceptable in commissioned work.
Hedge to match evidence strength. Declare limitations in the method and conclusions, not in a
footnote.

## Failure modes

Chronological lab-notes narrative instead of themes · "we surveyed the market" with no
instrument, sample or dates · interpretation leaking into findings · "might consider"
recommendations · orphan visuals · inflated front matter · mixed citation styles · buried
limitations · business plan with only the optimistic case.

## Original worked example

A Mbarara dairy cooperative commissions a feasibility study on a milk-cooling centre.
- Type: feasibility, formal (external funder, capital commitment).
- Criteria agreed up front: daily intake of at least 8,000 litres, payback under four years,
  grid reliability, and member willingness to deliver twice daily.
- Each option (build, lease, joint venture with a processor) is scored against those criteria in
  one table; the recommendation follows the scores, and the sensitivity case shows the decision
  holds if the farm-gate price falls by 10%.

## Ship checklist

- [ ] One report type chosen; structure matches it.
- [ ] Front and back matter scaled to length and formality.
- [ ] Executive summary stands alone.
- [ ] Method replicable.
- [ ] Findings, conclusions and recommendations separated.
- [ ] Every visual titled, captioned, sourced and referenced in text.
- [ ] One citation style; references complete.
- [ ] Limitations declared.
- [ ] Contents, cross-references and page numbers regenerated after the final edit.
- [ ] Classification marking present.
