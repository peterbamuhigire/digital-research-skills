# Report and Proposal Structure

Use this guide when the engine must turn research into a business report (inform, explain,
recommend) or a proposal (persuade a buyer or sponsor to authorise work). For choosing among
report types and the full formal-research and business-plan templates, load
`report-type-selection-and-formal-templates.md`. For sentence-level prose, load
`../../business-writing/references/plain-business-prose-and-tone.md`.

## Inputs to collect before drafting

| Input | Why it matters | Block drafting if missing? |
|---|---|---|
| Named primary reader and their decision | Structure follows the decision, not the research | Yes |
| Purpose statement (template below) | Tests whether the document has a job | Yes |
| Evidence register with source IDs | Every finding must trace to a source | Yes |
| Commissioning brief, terms of reference or RFP | Sets mandatory order, headings and exclusions | Yes for bids |
| Required template or house style | A wrong format can fail procurement before the decider reads it | No, but flag |
| Budget, price or cost envelope | Recommendations and proposals are incomplete without cost | Yes for proposals |

**Purpose statement template.** "After reading this [report/proposal], [primary reader] will
[decide/approve/fund/change] X, because the document shows Y, and will take action Z by [date]."
If X, Y or Z is vague, planning is incomplete.

## Decision rules

1. **Report or proposal?** A report looks backward and at the present, and its surface is
   evidence-led and neutral. A proposal looks forward, is benefit-led, and competes. If the
   reader's decision is "accept the findings", write a report; if it is "award, sign or fund",
   write a proposal.
2. **Hybrid (investigative proposal).** Use report structure for the diagnosis and proposal
   structure for the response; mark the join with a heading. Never let persuasion leak into the
   findings.
3. **Map the reader set.** For each role (decider, influencer, blocker such as finance,
   day-to-day user, gatekeeper such as procurement) record what they care about, what they must
   see, and their likely objection. The decider shapes page one; the others are served by
   structure (appendices, compliance matrix, transition plan), not by changing tone.
4. **Answer first.** Lead each document, and each section, with its conclusion. A
   recommendation that first appears on page 14 has been buried.
5. **Findings, conclusions, recommendations stay separate.** Findings describe; conclusions
   interpret without new evidence; recommendations instruct (owner, date, cost).
6. **Cut rule.** If deleting a paragraph would not change the recommendation, move it to an
   appendix or delete it.

## Procedure

1. **Plan.** Write the purpose statement and fill the reader map.
2. **Skeleton pass.** Descriptive headings plus one line per section. Get sponsor sign-off on
   the skeleton before writing prose.
3. **Argument pass.** Write the first sentence of every paragraph. The document should already
   work as a skim.
4. **Evidence pass.** Add support, visuals and citations under each claim.
5. **Executive summary.** Write it last, from the finished body.
6. **Polish pass.** Language, rhythm, formatting.
7. **Proof in separate passes.** Structural, logical, linguistic, mechanical. Do the mechanical
   pass on paper, aloud, or paragraph by paragraph from the end.
8. **Cold-reader test.** Someone without context restates the recommendation and its three main
   reasons after one read. If they cannot, restructure.

## Structures

### Long report (10 to 40 pages)

Title page (informative title, author, recipient, date, version, classification) → contents
(decimal numbering, at most three levels) → executive summary → introduction (why the report
exists, scope, exclusions, terms of reference, brief method) → findings grouped by theme, each
as claim, evidence, implication → conclusions in priority order → numbered recommendations
(action, owner, date, cost, rank) → implementation outline → references → appendices (data,
method detail, calculations, glossary). The body carries most of the length; the introduction
and conclusions stay short.

### Short internal report (up to 5 pages)

Purpose · background · findings · conclusions · recommendations · next steps.

### Status report

RAG status · achievements against plan · variances · risks and issues with owners · decisions
needed · next-period plan.

### External proposal

1. Cover letter: one page, names the buyer's problem in their words, asks for the next step.
2. Executive summary that sells: their problem, the approach, the outcome, price band, why you.
3. Understanding of need: mirror their language and quantify the cost of inaction.
4. Objectives framed as the client's measurable outcomes, not your activities.
5. Approach in phases, with the reason each phase beats the alternative.
6. Deliverables with acceptance criteria and explicit in-scope and out-of-scope lists.
7. Timeline with client dependencies marked.
8. Team: named people, role, allocation, one-line credential.
9. Price and terms: fixed, time-and-materials or capped; milestone-linked payments; expenses;
   validity period.
10. Assumptions, risks and dependencies, each risk paired with a mitigation.
11. Evidence of fit: two or three comparable engagements with measured outcomes.
12. Acceptance: signature block, contract reference, named contact.
13. Appendices: CVs, certifications, draft contract.

At every section, answer the buyer's silent question: what does this mean for me?

### Internal proposal or business case

Recommendation · strategic context · problem and cost of inaction · options considered ·
preferred option · costs and benefits (NPV, payback) · risks · implementation · decision
requested.

### Bid response to an RFP

Mirror the RFP order exactly. Put the compliance matrix first and the executive summary second,
then the mandated sections, then appendices. Where the RFP is silent, use the external-proposal
order.

### Executive briefing (1 to 2 pages)

Headline decision · why now · three supporting facts · risk envelope · ask and deadline.

## Executive summary rules

- It stands alone: assume it is the only part the decider reads.
- One page, usually 250 to 400 words; two pages only for documents over 50 pages.
- Four moves: context (one or two sentences), core finding or proposition, three to five
  evidence headlines, the ask.
- Nothing appears in the summary that is not expanded in the body. No hedging verbs.

## Persuasion inside a report or proposal

Use situation, complication, question, answer: agreed background, what changed, the question
that follows, and the answer supported by three to five mutually exclusive reasons, each with
evidence. Surface the strongest objection and answer it; silence reads as concession. Load
`../../executive-communication/references/scqa-opener.md` for the opener pattern.

## Headings, language and visuals

- Descriptive headings that carry the point ("Collection costs rose 18% in Q3", not "Costs");
  parallel grammar within a level.
- Plain words; average sentence 15 to 20 words; paragraphs of three to five sentences led by a
  topic sentence; active voice unless the agent is irrelevant.
- Reports use third person or "we recommend"; proposals use "you will receive" and "we will
  deliver".
- Chart for trend, comparison or proportion; table where exact values matter; prose when three
  numbers fit a sentence. Every visual has a takeaway title, a source line and a sentence in the
  text that points to it. Load `../../executive-communication/references/chart-selection-by-message.md`.

## Failure modes

Data dump without synthesis · buried recommendation · capability list not mapped to the buyer's
need · "consider" or "explore" recommendations without owner, date or cost · method pages
padding thin findings · credentials before the client's problem · every sentence hedged ·
figures that differ between summary and body · wrong template · no closing ask · no risks named ·
a previous client's name left in reused text · passive voice hiding who decided.

## Premium versus generic output

| Generic AI output | Senior professional output |
|---|---|
| "This report explores various aspects of..." | Opens with the decision and the number that drives it |
| Recommendations as verbs without owners | "Finance to renegotiate the fuel contract by 30 Nov; saving UGX 84m a year" |
| Every section the same length and shape | Length follows evidentiary weight |
| Risks omitted to look confident | Risks named, sized and mitigated |

## Original worked example

A Kampala logistics firm asks whether to replace its diesel delivery fleet with electric
motorcycles for last-mile routes.

- Purpose: "After reading this, the operations director will approve a 12-bike pilot on the
  Ntinda and Kireka routes, because running-cost evidence shows a 14-month payback, and will
  sign the lease by 15 November."
- Page one leads with the pilot recommendation, the payback figure, and the one risk that could
  break it (battery-swap station coverage east of Kireka).
- Findings are grouped by theme (cost per delivery, uptime, rider acceptance), not by the order
  in which interviews happened.
- Recommendation 2 reads: "Fleet manager to agree a swap-station service level with the
  supplier before lease signature; walk away if coverage is below 90% of route kilometres."

## Ship checklist

- [ ] Purpose statement written and met.
- [ ] Executive summary stands alone and matches the body.
- [ ] Answer or proposition on page one.
- [ ] Recommendations numbered, owned, dated, costed and ranked.
- [ ] Every claim sourced; figures reconcile across summary, body and appendices.
- [ ] Every visual has a takeaway title, source line and text anchor.
- [ ] Headings descriptive, parallel, at most three levels.
- [ ] Objections answered (proposal) or limitations stated (report).
- [ ] Each reader-map role served.
- [ ] Risks, price validity and acceptance mechanism present (proposal).
- [ ] Cold-reader test passed; proof passes done separately.
- [ ] Template compliance, version, classification and distribution checked.
- [ ] Final page states the next action and deadline.
